General Relativity Kerr Spacetime & EHT Equations

1. Kerr Metric Tensor (Boyer-Lindquist Coordinates)

● RUNNING

Describes exact spacetime geometry around a spinning black hole with mass \(M\) and spin angular momentum \(a^*\). Describes frame dragging and ergosphere deformation.

2. Kerr Frame Dragging Angular Velocity

● RUNNING

Spacetime itself is dragged around by the spinning black hole. Objects inside the ergosphere are forced to rotate in the direction of black hole spin.

3. Kerr Innermost Stable Circular Orbit (ISCO)

● RUNNING

For non-spinning Schwarzschild black holes, \(r_{\text{ISCO}} = 6 M\). For maximally spinning Kerr black holes (\(a^* \to 0.998\)), the ISCO moves inward to \(r_{\text{ISCO}} \to 1 M\)!

4. Kerr Ergosphere & Outer Horizon

● RUNNING

The ergosphere boundary \(r_E(\theta)\) touches the outer event horizon \(r_+\) at the poles and bulges outward at the equator to \(2M\). In this region, spacetime drags matter faster than light!

5. Lense-Thirring Geodesic Deflection Force

● RUNNING

Spin-orbit coupling produces a lateral force on light rays, making prograde rays experience stronger deflection than retrograde rays, bending the shadow into a characteristic asymmetric D-shape.

6. Gravitational Time Dilation & Redshift

● RUNNING

Clocks near the event horizon run infinitely slow relative to distant observers. Light emitted close to \(r_s\) loses energy, shifting toward infinite redshift.

7. Shakura-Sunyaev Accretion Disk Profile

● RUNNING

Standard thin accretion disk radiative temperature profile \(T(r) \propto r^{-3/4}\) with zero-torque boundary condition at the innermost stable circular orbit \(r_{\text{in}} = r_{\text{ISCO}}\).

8. M87* Blandford-Znajek Relativistic Jet Power

IDLE (Jet OFF)

Extracts rotational energy from a spinning Kerr black hole via magnetic field lines, launching collimated plasma jets accelerating to \(>0.99 c\)!

9. Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) Angular Diameter

● RUNNING

Observed shadow size on Earth: Sagittarius A* subtends \(51.8\ \mu\text{as}\), while M87* subtends \(42.0\ \mu\text{as}\) across 55 million light-years.

10. Relativistic Doppler Color Shift (Visible Spectrum)

IDLE

In the optical band, relativistic orbital velocity transforms emission frequency directly: Approaching gas shifts into blue/UV (νobs > νemit), while receding gas drops into deep red/infrared.

11. Relativistic Synchrotron Beaming Law

● RUNNING

In radio (EHT 1.3mm) and X-ray corona bands, non-thermal synchrotron radiation beams as Iν ∝ δ3+α, boosting the approaching horn by factors of 10× to 100×!

12. Earth VLBI Diffraction Limit & Real EHT Photo Blur

● EHT OBSERVATION

Why do Google/NASA press photos look like a blurry orange donut? The Event Horizon Telescope synthesizes an Earth-diameter virtual aperture (D ≈ 10,000 km) at λ = 1.3 mm. By the diffraction limit θ ≈ 1.22 λ / D, its angular resolution is limited to ~20 μas. Because the shadow ring is only ~45 μas across, Earth's telescope beam naturally blurs the razor-sharp photon ring into the famous fuzzy donut! 13. Synchrotron Magnetic Linear Polarization (IXPE)

IDLE

In the IXPE polarimetry band, non-thermal synchrotron emission has up to ~72% linear polarization. Electric Vector Position Angles (EVPA) trace helical magnetic field topologies threading the ergosphere and launching relativistic jets.

14. Next-Gen EHT (345 GHz) Sub-Millimeter Resolution

IDLE

By moving up to 345 GHz (λ = 0.87 mm) with orbiting space satellite dishes, ngEHT doubles angular resolution to ~10 μas, separating the razor-thin n=1 photon sub-ring from turbulent accretion disk emission.

15. Space VLBI Earth-Moon Sub-Microarcsecond Diffraction

IDLE

A lunar orbital radio antenna provides an unprecedented baseline of 384,000 km, reaching sub-microarcsecond resolution (≤ 0.85 μas) to resolve individual second-order photon loops (n=2) orbiting the event horizon.

16. Quasar Eddington Radiation Limit (TON 618)

IDLE

The maximum luminosity an accretion disk can radiate before outward radiation pressure halts gravitational infall. For TON 618 (66 billion solar masses), the Eddington limit is a staggering 8.3 × 1041 W (140 trillion solar luminosities).
